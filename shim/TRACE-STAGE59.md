# Stage59: IRQ-independent CPU0 SGI1 readiness recovery

**Device:** Redmi 6A `cactus`, MT6761/MT6765, GICv3. **Hardware Stage59:** pending.

Stage58 flashed and its framebuffer reported `ST58 SGI1 DISABLED` and
`CACTUS GICv3 pre-rearm status=0x3ff00000040 irq_seen=0`. Bit `0x40`
means redistributor SGI1 is disabled; ICC_HPPIR1 gave spurious `0x3ff` and
no SGI1 IRQ was observed. No evidence yet establishes whether the initial
GICR write was ignored or the path later cleared SGI1. `IOSleep(1)` can
also deadlock the 250ms recovery timer if the XNU timer interrupt is absent.

Stage59 patch `xnu/patches/0096-cactus-sgi-wait-stage59.patch` is generated by
`tools/stage59_generate_patch.py` against the Stage58-patched source. It:

- starts the first SGI1 re-arm immediately after CPU0 initialization;
- uses a 100µs `IODelay` while boot CPU is initialized but signal-disabled,
  preserving existing bounded retries and timeout;
- logs GICR enable/waker after initial GIC setup and after recovery;
- waits no more than 10ms for GICR wake before touching its SGI registers;
- reports `ST59 SGI1 enabled` or `ST59 SGI1 enable not latched` on framebuffer.

These repairs preserve Group-1 GICv3 semantics, XNU ready-state checks, the
Stage51 native display, and signed Stage55 static Mach-O diagnostic PID1
RAMDisk (`rd=md0`). The user's iOS 16 IPSW is **not** launched here.

-CaptureSeconds 120` uses serial/product and image guards, then captures

The final native framebuffer `ST59` status, followed by `ST57 CPU0 init and
IRQ OK`/later Stage56 or BSD marks if present, is needed to determine whether
the SGI enable write latched and whether XNU progressed. Compilation and
USB prehandoff alone do not prove IRQ recovery, BSD mount, or PID1 execution.
